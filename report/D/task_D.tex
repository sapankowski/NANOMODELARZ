\subsection{Purpose}

Task D evaluates the harmonic lattice dynamics of the relaxed magnetic
ground-state structures identified in Task B.  For Ni, the phonon calculation
uses the relaxed ferromagnetic PBE structure.  For NiO, the calculation uses
the relaxed DFT+\(U\) AFM-II structure, because this is the lowest-energy NiO
state considered in Task B.

\subsection{Computational Setup}

The phonon calculations use the finite-difference force-constant approach in
VASP.  In this approach, VASP displaces atoms in a supercell, computes the
resulting forces, constructs the second-order force constants, and diagonalizes
the dynamical matrix.  The Ni finite-difference setup uses \texttt{IBRION = 5},
\texttt{NFREE = 2}, \texttt{POTIM = 0.015}~\AA, and \texttt{ISYM = 0}.  The NiO
force-constant calculation uses \texttt{IBRION = 6} and \texttt{ISYM = 2}, so
VASP reduces the DFT+\(U\) finite displacements using the magnetic-cell
symmetry \cite{vasp-phonon-fd}.  This choice was the one that ran successfully
for the primitive magnetic cell, although \texttt{ISYM = 0} would be the safer
setting for a more exhaustive displaced-supercell study of an AFM+\(U\) system.

The Ni calculation uses a \(2\times2\times2\) phonon supercell constructed from
the relaxed Task B cell.  For Ni, the relaxed conventional fcc cell is first
converted to the one-atom primitive fcc cell; the phonon supercell therefore
contains 8 Ni atoms and uses an \(8\times8\times8\) electronic k-point mesh.  The
NiO finite-difference calculation uses the 4-ion AFM-II magnetic primitive cell
and a \(16\times16\times16\) electronic k-point mesh.
Following the VASP NiO DFT+\(U\) tutorial convention, the two magnetic Ni
sublattices are represented as separate formal species, \texttt{Ni\_up} and
\texttt{Ni\_down}; the corresponding POTCAR therefore contains two Ni PAW
datasets followed by the O PAW dataset \cite{vasp-nio-u}.
These k-point meshes preserve approximately the same reciprocal-space sampling
density as the smaller cells used in Tasks B and C.  The phonon dispersion and
phonon density of states are obtained by reading the calculated force constants
from \texttt{vaspout.h5}, supplying a \texttt{QPOINTS} path or mesh, and setting
\texttt{LPHON\_DISPERSION} or \texttt{PHON\_DOS}, respectively
\cite{vasp-phonon-dispersion}.

The NiO force-constant step is preceded by a static calculation of the same
magnetic cell.  The resulting \texttt{CHGCAR} and \texttt{WAVECAR} are then
used to initialize the finite-difference calculation, which avoids starting the
DFT+\(U\) displaced-cell force evaluations from atomic densities.  A larger
32-ion \(2\times2\times2\) NiO supercell was tested, but on the available VASP
build the static DFT+\(U\) initialization repeatedly terminated with a
\texttt{SIGFPE} before the first electronic iteration.  The primitive-cell setup
therefore provides a runnable finite-difference calculation consistent with the
standard AFM-II NiO cell used in the VASP DFT+\(U\) examples.
The larger-cell failure was not fully diagnosed.  Plausible causes include a
\texttt{MAGMOM} sequence that did not follow the 32-ion atom order, inconsistent
\texttt{Ni\_up}/\texttt{Ni\_down}/O ordering between \texttt{POSCAR} and
\texttt{POTCAR}, incomplete DFT+\(U\) arrays such as using
\texttt{LDAUL = 2 -1} rather than \texttt{LDAUL = 2 2 -1} for separate
magnetic Ni species, an unsafe symmetry reduction of displaced AFM+\(U\)
structures, or incompatible \texttt{WAVECAR}/\texttt{CHGCAR} restart files from
a different cell.

For NiO, a separate primitive-cell linear-response calculation with
\texttt{LEPSILON = .TRUE.} is included to compute the ion-clamped dielectric
tensor and Born effective charge tensors.  These quantities are passed to the
phonon runs through \texttt{LPHON\_POLAR}, \texttt{PHON\_DIELECTRIC}, and
\texttt{PHON\_BORN\_CHARGES} so that the long-range dipole contribution and
LO-TO splitting can be treated in the polar oxide \cite{vasp-phonon-dispersion,
vasp-lepsilon}.

\subsection{Results}

The Task D VASP calculations completed successfully for both systems.  The Ni
calculation used the ferromagnetic PBE force constants, while the NiO
calculation used the DFT+\(U\) AFM-II force constants with the polar correction
from the Born effective charges and electronic dielectric tensor.  The
resulting \(\Gamma\)-point phonon summaries are given in
Tables~\ref{tab:task-d-summary} and~\ref{tab:task-d-gamma}.  No imaginary modes
were found in either system.  The small values reported as
\(0.000~\mathrm{THz}\) are numerical acoustic modes at \(\Gamma\), not
physical instabilities.

\subsection{Discussion}

The calculated phonon spectra do not show any imaginary modes, so both relaxed
ground-state structures are dynamically stable within the harmonic
approximation used here.  For Ni, the three \(\Gamma\)-point modes are the
expected acoustic modes of the one-atom primitive fcc cell and are numerically
zero.  The dispersion and PhDOS therefore support the stability of the relaxed
ferromagnetic PBE Ni structure.  Because the primitive fcc cell contains only
one atom, there are no optical branches for Ni at \(\Gamma\); the entire
spectrum consists of the three acoustic branches.  This makes the absence of
negative frequencies a direct check that the relaxed ferromagnetic structure is
not sitting on a harmonic lattice instability.

For NiO, the three acoustic \(\Gamma\)-point modes are also zero within
numerical precision, and the optical modes are all positive.  This supports the
dynamical stability of the DFT+\(U\) AFM-II NiO structure used as the Task B
ground state.  The presence of nine optical modes follows from the four-ion
magnetic primitive cell: \(3N=12\) total modes, three acoustic and nine optical.
The highest optical modes are dominated by the lighter oxygen sublattice, while
the lower-frequency modes contain more motion of the heavier Ni atoms.  The
calculated NiO optical frequencies span roughly \(192\)--\(569~\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\),
which is the correct broad scale for NiO optical lattice vibrations observed in
infrared, Raman, and neutron studies, although those experiments also show
spin-phonon effects and temperature-dependent splittings that are outside the
present harmonic calculation \cite{lacerda-nio-phonon,sun-nio-phonon}.  The
polar correction was included for NiO because the oxide is ionic; this is
important for the long-range dipole contribution and LO--TO splitting near
\(\Gamma\).

The main limitation of the present NiO calculation is that it uses the
4-ion magnetic primitive cell rather than a larger phonon supercell, because
the tested 32-ion DFT+\(U\) supercell repeatedly triggered a \texttt{SIGFPE}
during the initial SCF setup on the available VASP build.  Therefore the NiO
phonon dispersion and optical frequencies should not be treated as fully
converged quantitative predictions.  They are useful as a stability check for
the selected AFM-II structure, but a production-quality phonon study should
debug the 32-ion setup, rerun with the correct AFM species/order and DFT+\(U\)
arrays, preferably use \texttt{ISYM = 0} for displaced magnetic structures, and
then test the convergence of the force constants with supercell size.
